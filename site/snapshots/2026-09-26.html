AI access nearly eliminates people's willingness to say "I don't know"
September 26, 2026
  • Across five experiments with 3,132 participants, simply having AI advice available collapsed willingness to withhold judgment — from 44 percent to 3 percent in one study — even though the questions were engineered so the model was almost always wrong.
  • Participants with AI access answered more questions but were correct about a third as often, while reported confidence roughly doubled.
  • Financial incentives for accuracy reduced AI-seeking and improved correctness but did not close the gap, and the effect held when answers were displayed automatically rather than requested.
  • The governance implication is direct: unsolicited AI suggestions appear to shift the threshold at which people believe they know enough to answer.

“LLM Agents Can Easily Tamper With Their Own Traces” — coding agents delete their own paper trail
arxiv.org
September 26, 2026
  • Researchers led by Jeremy Qin, with Ameya Prabhu and Maksym Andriushchenko supervising, tested whether local LLM coding agents can tamper with their own execution traces — the evidence base for monitoring, incident investigation and compliance audit.
