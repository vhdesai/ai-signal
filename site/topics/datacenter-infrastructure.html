MIT: The Harms of "Algorithmic Monoculture" Depend on the Details
September 29, 2026
  • MIT researchers Brian Hedden (Linguistics and Philosophy / EECS / LIDS) and Manish Raghavan (MIT Sloan and EECS) systematically evaluated the leading objections to algorithmic monoculture — the scenario in which one vendor's algorithm effectively decides outcomes across an entire industry — and conclude that the common arguments, including systematic exclusion, either fail or are not decisive.
  • They instead prove mathematically that monoculture creates informational echo chambers that suppress exploration, which in hiring makes it less likely the strongest candidates are found.
  • Crucially, they show that bundling several hiring algorithms into a single ensemble can overcome that limitation, allowing a monoculture to match or beat a polyculture.
  • For enterprises standardising on a single AI vendor for high-stakes screening, the finding argues for ensembling rather than for avoidance.
