EAGLE Model Detects 90% of Esophageal Cancers on Routine Non-Contrast Chest CT
September 28, 2026
  • EAGLE (Esophageal AI-Guided malignant Lesion Evaluation) was trained on 6,813 patients at two Chinese centres and validated across 12 centres in China, the Czech Republic and Australia covering 80,612 patients.
  • In external testing across eight centres (n=11,466) it reached 90.0% sensitivity for cancer at 98.5% specificity, though sensitivity fell to 60.1% for stage 1 disease and 52.5% for high-grade precancerous lesions.
  • Recalibration on 35,402 real-world patients cut false positives 72.7%; prospective testing in 17,446 patients showed a 42.2% positive predictive value.
  • The commercial relevance is opportunistic screening — extracting additional diagnoses from scans already performed, with no extra radiation or procedure. nature.com — Large-scale esophageal cancer screening through noncontrast CT

MIT uses small-data machine learning to make RNA vaccines survive room temperature for a year
news.mit.edu
September 28, 2026
  • MIT engineers working with MIT CSAIL applied a machine-learning algorithm purpose-built for very small datasets to find excipient formulations that stabilize the lipid nanoparticles delivering mRNA vaccines.
  • The algorithm screened roughly 50 FDA-approved excipients and converged “in just a handful of iterations, rather than the exhaustive search that would normally be required,” per CSAIL assistant professor Mina Konaković Luković.
  • The resulting vaccines remained stable at room temperature for a year, or at ~98°F for two months, and produced immune responses in mice equivalent to a Moderna-like control.
  • The work appears in Nature Biotechnology, with Ana Jaklenec and Robert Langer as senior authors.

Sakana AI and the University of Tokyo raise robot task success from 25% to 73% with test-time search
sakana.ai
September 28, 2026
  • SAIL (Scaling In-Context Imitation Learning) treats robot control as Monte Carlo tree search over whole trajectories: a frozen vision-language model generates candidate motions from retrieved demonstrations, a simulator executes them, and an evaluator VLM returns step-level feedback for refinement.
  • Across six simulated manipulation tasks, raising the search budget from one node to 45 lifted average success from 25% to 73%, peaking at 95% on one task, using Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5 with no weight updates.
  • Physical validation was narrow — five of six trials on a single block-placement task — and execution is open-loop, so the deployment question remains open.
  • Accepted to IROS 2026.
  • Academic Research ACADEMIC
University of Tokyo work anchors the inference-compute-for-reliability thesis in robotics
pub.sakana.ai
September 28, 2026
  • The SAIL authors are Makoto Sato and Yusuke Iwasawa (University of Tokyo) with Yujin Tang and So Kuroki (Sakana AI), the work conducted during Sato's internship.
  • The methodological claim matters beyond robotics: the LLM playbook of spending inference compute instead of retraining now shows measurable transfer to physical action, with search cost absorbed in simulation rather than on hardware.
  • The paper publishes no wall-clock or compute-cost figures for the added search budget, which is the gating variable for any real control loop.
