# Princeton "Shadow Evaluations": AI Agents Fail to Make Research Progress on Genuinely Open Problems

A Princeton-led study introduced "shadow evaluations," where AI agents attempt genuinely open research problems from unpublished papers. The original paper authors then grade the outputs, avoiding the benchmark distortion that happens when answers are already known. Across two unpublished NeurIPS submissions, frontier agents completed engineering work but failed to make real scientific progress. Both resulting papers were clearly rejected by the original authors, despite six days and thousands of dollars in compute. The study is a strong counterweight to narratives that current agents are close to automating frontier research or recursive AI R&D. Links: arXiv
