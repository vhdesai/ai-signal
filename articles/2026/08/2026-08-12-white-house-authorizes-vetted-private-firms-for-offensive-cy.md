# White House Authorizes Vetted Private Firms for Offensive Cyber Operations

A presidential memorandum directs the National Coordination Center to let vetted U.S. companies conduct cyber-surveillance and “cyber-effects” operations against foreign criminal groups, under joint DOJ and DHS oversight. Participants face contractual vetting, detailed operating procedures, full legal-compliance obligations, $1M bonds, and explicit exclusions on the most severe outcomes. The framework is the first formal delegation of offensive cyber capability to private-sector actors at this scale. As AI lowers the cost of autonomous offensive operations, the infrastructure for legitimate private-sector cyber action is being codified alongside it. Expect both security-vendor interest and civil-liberties scrutiny as the program operationalizes. GovInfoSecurity | White House POLICY
