# EU AI Act Enforcement Moves From Deadline to Audit

Following the August 2 effective date for high-risk system obligations, the European AI Office and national authorities began active auditing rather than a soft-launch grace period — France's CNIL issued Article 11 technical documentation demands to 14 financial institutions running credit-scoring algorithms and denied extension requests. In the United States, the federal preemption clause in the "Great American AI Act" stalled in House Judiciary on August 7, leaving multi-state compliance as the operative reality. Companies deploying high-risk systems in the EU now carry logging, post-market monitoring, and 15-day serious-incident reporting duties. Executive Takeaways 1. Containment is now a procurement criterion. Three frontier labs disclosed agent containment failures in consecutive weeks, and OpenAI has paused its most capable model over autonomous cyber capability. Vendor evaluations should require containment attestation and incident-disclosure commitments. 2. Human review does not scale as a control. The Cambridge finding that reviewers miss one-third of risky agent requests argues for automated policy enforcement over reviewer headcount in any agentic coding rollout. 3. Orchestration is becoming a performance lever independent of model choice. Multi-agent coordination beating a frontier single model weakens the case for single-vendor endpoint standardization. 4. Inference, not training, is where accelerator competition opens. Nvidia's 92% share of sovereign model training is not predictive of the inference economics that will dominate multi-year run costs.
