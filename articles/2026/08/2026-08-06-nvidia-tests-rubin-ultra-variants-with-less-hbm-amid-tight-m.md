# Nvidia tests Rubin Ultra variants with less HBM amid tight memory supply

Nvidia is testing configurations of its next-generation Rubin Ultra chip with less high-bandwidth memory than originally planned—a concession to supply-chain reality that validates memory, not compute, as the binding constraint on AI infrastructure scaling. The move could mean reduced per-chip performance ceilings but faster time-to-market and broader availability. For data center operators and cloud providers planning next-generation deployments, it reshapes capacity planning assumptions. The story reinforces a wider theme: even Nvidia's roadmap is now shaped more by supply-chain bottlenecks than by engineering ambition.
