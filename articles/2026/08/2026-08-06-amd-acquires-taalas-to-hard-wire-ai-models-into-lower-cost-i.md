# AMD acquires Taalas to hard-wire AI models into lower-cost inference silicon

AMD acquired Taalas, a Toronto startup that builds model-specific integrated circuits—chips that cast a model's weights and dataflow directly into transistors instead of shuttling them through high-bandwidth memory. The approach radically lowers inference cost: Taalas's first test chip, HC1, served Meta's Llama 3.1 8B at roughly 17,000 tokens per second—73 times faster than Nvidia's H200 at one-tenth the power consumption. A second chip, HC2, targets models around 20 billion parameters. The tradeoff is flexibility: each chip runs only the model it was built for, but Taalas says only two of its 100-plus fabrication layers change between designs, enabling roughly two-month tape-out cycles that keep iteration costs low. AMD plans to integrate Taalas silicon alongside its Instinct GPUs, Helios racks, and Epyc processors, giving customers a purpose-built, lower-cost inference option that sidesteps the GPU pricing and HBM supply constraints dominating the market. Founded by former Tenstorrent CEO Ljubisa Bajic, Taalas had raised $219 million from Quiet Capital, Fidelity, and Pierre Lamond. This is AMD's third AI acquisition in nine months—following MK1 and Mext—and its most direct challenge yet to Nvidia's inference dominance.
