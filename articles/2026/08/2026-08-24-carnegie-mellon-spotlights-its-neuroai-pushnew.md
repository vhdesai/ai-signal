# Carnegie Mellon spotlights its NeuroAI pushNew

CMU detailed interdisciplinary NeuroAI work in which neuroscientists use state-of-the-art ML models to understand how the brain perceives sights and sounds and processes language, while AI researchers borrow principles from biological intelligence to build more adaptable machines. Assistant professor Maggie Henderson’s group combines fMRI with deep neural network representations to predict how the human visual cortex responds to images. The piece frames NeuroAI as a genuinely two-way exchange advancing both fields.
