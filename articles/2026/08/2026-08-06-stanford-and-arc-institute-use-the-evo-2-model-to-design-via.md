# Stanford and Arc Institute use the Evo 2 model to design viable bacteriophages from scratch

Researchers at Stanford and the Arc Institute used the Evo 2 genome language model to design bacteriophages, experimentally testing 285 AI-generated designs and obtaining 16 viable, replicating viruses with novel genomes — some overcoming bacterial resistance that natural phages could not. Published in Science, it is the first demonstration of generative AI designing functional viral genomes, pointing toward AI-designed phage therapeutics against drug-resistant bacteria. The team excluded human/animal/plant pathogens from training data, but the dual-use biosecurity implications drew immediate attention.
