# The EU AI Act has now moved from theory to enforceable operating reality for general-purpose AI providers

The EU AI Act has now moved from theory to enforceable operating reality for general-purpose AI providers. - With powers to demand evaluations, restrict access, and levy meaningful fines, Brussels is no longer just setting norms but actively shaping market behavior. - Transparency requirements around chatbot disclosure and deepfake labeling also raise the compliance bar for downstream product teams, not only model labs. - The large number of organizations signing the Code of Practice suggests market participants recognize the cost of non-alignment in Europe. - For multinational executives, AI governance now requires live compliance programs with auditability, documentation, and product-level implementation plans.
