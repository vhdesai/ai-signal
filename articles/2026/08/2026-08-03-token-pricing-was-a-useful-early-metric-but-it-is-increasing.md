# Token pricing was a useful early metric, but it is increasingly too narrow to capture the economics of real AI systems…

Token pricing was a useful early metric, but it is increasingly too narrow to capture the economics of real AI systems in production. - Actual cost depends on routing logic, infrastructure utilization, orchestration overhead, latency targets, failure rates, and the human work displaced or accelerated. - That shifts AI management toward a FinOps-style discipline where finance and engineering need shared visibility into workload value and operating efficiency. - Reliability also matters economically because repeated retries, escalation paths, and manual review loops can erase nominal model-price advantages. - Boards and CFOs will increasingly demand unit economics tied to workflow outcomes rather than abstract cost-per-token comparisons.
