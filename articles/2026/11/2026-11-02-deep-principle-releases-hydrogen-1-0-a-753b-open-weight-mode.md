# Deep Principle releases Hydrogen-1.0, a 753B open-weight model for scientific research

Hangzhou-based Deep Principle built Hydrogen-1.0 by further training Z.ai's GLM-5.3, a mixture-of-experts model with about 40B active parameters and a 1M-token context window. It was trained on chemistry, materials, and life-sciences data, electronic lab notebook records, and records of discovery attempts, including failures. The weights are released under the MIT License and run on eight GPUs. The company reports 38.3% on FrontierScience-Research versus 33.3% for GPT-6-Astra, and 93.0% on GPQA-Diamond; all results are the company's own, and it has not released its datasets or training recipes. Notably, the company excluded GPT-6-Astra and Opus 5.5 from its experiment-planning test because they refused more than half the requests.
