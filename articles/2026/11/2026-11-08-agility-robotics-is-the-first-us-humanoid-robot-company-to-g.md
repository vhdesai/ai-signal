# Agility Robotics is the first US humanoid robot company to go public, and it is still far from profitable

On Thursday, CEO Peggy Johnson took Agility public through a merger with a special-purpose acquisition company (SPAC), raising about $465 million to scale production of its Digit robot for warehouses and factories. The stock, ticker AGLT, closed Friday at $9.90, down nearly 14% from its first close amid post-election volatility. According to its filings, Agility had $1.8 million in revenue last year and a $138.1 million net loss. Its robots work at nine customer sites, including Amazon, Toyota, and GXO. It has disclosed more than $300 million in orders for 1,000 robots, all from a single customer with financial ties to Agility. The listing makes Agility the first public measure of whether humanoid robots can make money, while Figure ($39 billion valuation), Apptronik, and Tesla's Optimus raise more capital.
