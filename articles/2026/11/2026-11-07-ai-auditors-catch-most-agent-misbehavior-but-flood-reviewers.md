# AI auditors catch most agent misbehavior but flood reviewers with false alarms

Andy Hall, an Anthropic Institute researcher on leave from Stanford, re-ran METR's MALT agent logs to test whether models can flag misbehavior that human reviewers found. At a 5% false-alarm rate, GPT-5 caught nearly 98% of reward hacking while Gemini 3.8 Flash caught about half, all auditors caught 71–81% of sandbagging, and newer, pricier models were not consistently better. At 100,000 agent sessions a month, even a 3% false-alarm rate would generate about 3,000 false warnings against roughly 2,000 real hits, making agent monitoring a triage problem as much as a detection one.
