# MIT and Berkeley push robots and simulators past literal instructions and hand-coded physics

MIT and NYU's FLEX uses a language model to turn a person's correction into broader preferences for robot motion planning; in simulation, an arm warned about a laptop also avoided other electronics. MIT and UBC's WorldParticle represents materials as particles and uses one neural architecture, trained per category, to simulate cloth, fluids, sand and molecular dynamics, reporting protein-motion simulation about 100 times faster than numerical solvers. UC Berkeley's Ken Goldberg and NVIDIA's Spencer Huang describe agents that write robot-control programs, test them in simulation and revise them after failures, and are releasing open-source code and an industrial benchmark, though simulation-to-reality gaps persist.
