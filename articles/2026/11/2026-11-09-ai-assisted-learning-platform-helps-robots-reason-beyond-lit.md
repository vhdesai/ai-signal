# AI-assisted learning platform helps robots reason beyond literal commands

MIT and New York University researchers introduced FLEX, a motion-planning pipeline that uses natural-language corrections to infer broader user preferences. Its Add, Generalize and Mask stages add relevant environmental features, use GPT-4o to generalize them and remove features corresponding to absent objects; in simulations, a warning about a laptop generalized to avoiding other electronics. Evaluation used virtual robot arms and typed instructions, so the findings do not establish reliable household deployment or comprehensive safety.
