# MIT keeps AI models of chaotic systems within physically possible bounds

Researchers at MIT's Laboratory for Information and Decision Systems found a way to impose physics-based restrictions on machine-learning models of chaotic systems such as weather, climate and turbulent flows, so their predictions never drift beyond what is physically possible over time. The work by Sunbochen Tang, Navid Azizan and Themistoklis Sapsis is published in PNAS, and the team is making the supporting information freely available. Sapsis calls the lack of bounded-response guarantees a major obstacle that has held back data-driven models for prediction and control in aeronautics, hydrology and industrial processes.
