# CoRL research pushes robots past literal instructions and hand-engineering

MIT and NYU's FLEX uses a large language model to turn conversational corrections into broader preferences for robot motion planning; in simulation, an arm told not to spill on a laptop also steered clear of a nearby phone. UC Berkeley's Ken Goldberg and NVIDIA's Spencer Huang describe agentic robotics, in which AI agents write robot-control programs, test them in simulation and revise them after failures, and are releasing open-source code and an industrial benchmark. Both remain early: FLEX was tested on virtual arms, and a failed metal-bar trial in the Berkeley work shows that simulation-to-reality gaps persist.
