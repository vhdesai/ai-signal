# single AI model simulates the behavior of many substances, from sliding sand to folding proteins

Researchers at MIT, the University of British Columbia and collaborating institutions introduced WorldParticle, a neural architecture that represents materials as particles and learns their motion across different physical regimes. They demonstrated six phenomena and report protein-motion simulation approximately 100 times faster than numerical simulators, with particle merging used to reduce computational demands. These are researcher-reported results in demonstrated settings—not evidence of a universal physics simulator or validated performance across arbitrary scientific workloads.
