# Anthropic mathematician releases a Lean-verified Riemann hypothesis proof built with Claude

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge released a proof of the 1859 Riemann hypothesis, one of the Millennium Prize problems, written in the Lean proof language and developed with Claude. Independent checkers, including MIT's Andrew Sutherland, confirmed that it compiles. Alpöge says a human-readable explanation is still to come, and he separately released a Navier-Stokes result with NYU's Tristan Buckmaster. The same day, Stanford's Ravi Vakil posted an entirely human-written proof of the generalized Riemann hypothesis by postdoc Alessandro Masullo, which has not been formally checked. Machine-checked proofs are moving AI-assisted mathematics past disputes over credibility. Explaining those proofs to people is now the harder problem. Academic Research MIT SCIENTIFIC ML
