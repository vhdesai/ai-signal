# AI2's XYBench finds leading models rarely correct users' mistaken assumptions

The Allen Institute for AI, with Carnegie Mellon, Nvidia and Johns Hopkins, released XYBench, 8,115 real-world questions built on flawed assumptions, from programming to home repair. Leading models including Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5, Gemini 3.1 Pro and DeepSeek-V4 Pro often offered fixes but stayed anchored to the user's flawed approach, and all were less likely than human experts to redirect users to their real goal. The work won EMNLP 2026's Best Resource Paper Award and is a reminder that assistant quality depends on challenging the question, not just answering it.
