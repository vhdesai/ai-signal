# Anthropic study finds coordination costs when users delegate to separate agents

Anthropic Fellows research compared a shared coordinating agent with teams of agents acting for separate users in four simulated shared-resource environments. The coordinating approach performed better for the two highlighted Claude models, while mitigations such as explicit budget leadership and reading pending messages before checkout helped in particular settings. For enterprise deployments, the actionable lesson is to test coordination, consent, and resource controls rather than assume more agents produce better outcomes; these simulations do not establish that every multi-agent design is inferior.
