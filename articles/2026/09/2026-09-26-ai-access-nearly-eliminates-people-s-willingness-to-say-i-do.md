# AI access nearly eliminates people's willingness to say "I don't know"

Across five experiments with 3,132 participants, simply having AI advice available collapsed willingness to withhold judgment — from 44 percent to 3 percent in one study — even though the questions were engineered so the model was almost always wrong. Participants with AI access answered more questions but were correct about a third as often, while reported confidence roughly doubled. Financial incentives for accuracy reduced AI-seeking and improved correctness but did not close the gap, and the effect held when answers were displayed automatically rather than requested. The governance implication is direct: unsolicited AI suggestions appear to shift the threshold at which people believe they know enough to answer.
