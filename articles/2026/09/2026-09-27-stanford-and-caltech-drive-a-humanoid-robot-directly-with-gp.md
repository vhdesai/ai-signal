# Stanford and Caltech drive a humanoid robot directly with GPT-6 Astra

Researchers from Stanford and Caltech built HomeBody, a system that lets a Unitree G1 humanoid navigate an unfamiliar kitchen, tidy it and retrieve items from drawers. The design removes the trained control layer usually placed between a language model and the hardware: a swappable vision-language model — here GPT-6 Astra — calls directly into an extensible skill library for grasping, navigation and opening drawers. The robot first explores the space, builds a digital twin in Nvidia's Isaac Sim, and logs objects in spatial memory so it can locate items outside its field of view. Stated limitations are model latency, overheating finger servos and high compute cost. RESEARCH
