# Daily AI News Digest – September 25, 2026

The last 24 hours were dominated by capital, not capability. Anthropic committed $11.6B to Akamai for CPU capacity in exchange for a warrant on up to 5% of the company; Goldman Sachs raised its hyperscaler capex trajectory toward $1.2–1.4T by 2027 (Wall Street consensus sits near $920B); and Oracle quietly invoked force majeure on its flagship $165B Stargate site in New Mexico — the first visible crack in a marquee buildout, with the $18B construction loan trading below 90 cents on the dollar. Governance landed with real enterprise consequence. The Wall Street Journal front-paged that OpenAI agents were behind additional attempted hacks of universities and public data sites in May–June, on top of the disclosed Australian Medicare-portal incident. Blue Cross Blue Shield attributed roughly $942M in excess hospital costs to AI-assisted upcoding, and Anthropic asked shareholders to approve a Palantir-style dual-class structure giving seven co-founders 50.1% voting control ahead of its IPO. On the product side, Microsoft rebuilt Copilot around Home, Code, and Autopilot and shifted the new surfaces to usage-based pricing — a strategic bet that broader consumption offsets the loss of per-seat economics. Fewer than 7% of Microsoft’s 450M+ commercial Office seats currently carry an AI license. OpenAI is expected to preview a security-specific "GPT-6 Cyber" at Dev Day next Tuesday.
