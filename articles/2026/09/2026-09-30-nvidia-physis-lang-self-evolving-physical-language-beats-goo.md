# NVIDIA Physis-Lang: Self-Evolving Physical Language Beats Google Veo 3.1 on Physics Benchmarks

NVIDIA Research introduced Physis-Lang, a self-evolving symbolic language for physical reasoning that lifts Cosmos 3 past Google’s Veo 3.1 on standard physics-consistency benchmarks. The architecture — a physical-DSL trained jointly with the world model — materially closes NVIDIA’s video/world-model gap versus Google DeepMind and gives robotics and sim-to-real teams a directly applicable tool. Combined with the CoreWeave Rubin GA and the Stanford/Caltech HomeBody VLM-driven humanoid work last weekend, it is the third concrete data point in a week that general-purpose model architecture is moving toward tighter physical-world coupling. Executive Takeaways - Voluntary safety governance is the ceiling — enforcement will come through litigation, states, and vendor contracts. The White House "super intelligence" accord mirrors Biden-era voluntary commitments; Ted Cruz just blocked the federal AI safety review board; the OpenAI Hugging Face lawsuit and Anthropic’s S-1 existential-risk disclosure move the accountability conversation into court and prospectus. Bake explicit incident-notification SLAs, third-party evaluator rights, and indemnity carve-outs into every AI vendor agreement signed this quarter. - The AI IPO tape is already repricing. Public investors floated $1.5T for Anthropic against banker $2T talk; mid-sized tech IPOs are delaying on rate anxiety; Oracle’s Wisconsin campus is the second Stargate site under buildout stress in a week; OpenAI is at ~$70B annualized revenue but shelved a newer Astra on safety. Treat every 2026 AI IPO target valuation as a starting bid, not a floor, and watch Anthropic’s roadshow feedback as the calibrator for the next twelve months. - Cross-industry cyber-crisis coordination is now a CEO-level workstream. JPMorganChase, Citi, United, and Amazon are formalizing playbooks for cascading AI cyber failures and want to speak with "one voice" to frontier labs; only 14% of boards have a committee dedicated to cyber/geopolitical/AI risk. Elevate AI incident response from CISO to CEO-and-board dashboards, and treat vendor-side disclosure lag as a systemic risk (OpenAI acknowledged theirs; Anthropic, Meta, and Google show comparable behavior). - China is now attacking Nvidia’s CUDA moat and Anthropic just publicly credited Chinese frontier models with elite hacking capability. The DeepSeek–Huawei Ascend stack open-sourcing is the software-side complement to a year of hardware moves; GLM-5.3’s red-team capability reframes export controls from "chip access" to "capability parity." Multi-model procurement, egress governance, and clear policies on which Chinese-origin models can be routed through developer tooling belong on the CIO agenda this quarter, not next year. - The valuation cliff between AI-native and legacy software is now 3–5×. PitchBook has AI-native/AI-enabled software clearing 10–15× ARR while non-AI SaaS trades at 3–6×; software M&A is going underground as buyers refuse formal processes. If you own a SaaS asset without a legible AI narrative, the diligence conversation for any 2027 exit starts today — and Google’s pilot paying ~100 publishers for AI Overview contribution is the first Big Tech admission that the attribution-and-payments layer of the AI economy needs real dollars, not just link credit.
