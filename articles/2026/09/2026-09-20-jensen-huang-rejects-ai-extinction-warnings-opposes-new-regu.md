# Jensen Huang Rejects AI Extinction Warnings, Opposes New Regulation

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang told CBS News that predictions of AI-driven catastrophe are "doomsday narratives" not grounded in science: "2030 is not going to be the end of the world. There is 0% chance." He argued existing product-liability and unauthorized-access law is sufficient and that new AI-specific guardrails are unnecessary. Asked about Nvidia's incentives given a $5.3 trillion market value, Huang said the company's success is "directly connected to the safe deployment of products and services." He will attend the September 24 White House dinner for Xi Jinping and said he wants to discuss global AI standards. Executive Takeaways - Financing structure is now the AI risk story. SoftBank's record junk issuance and Nscale's 85% customer concentration are two views of the same exposure: capacity commitments underwritten by debt and a handful of counterparties, well ahead of delivered revenue. - Open-weight leverage is being re-priced. Step 5 Preview undercuts frontier pricing while Qwen quietly moved off Apache 2.0 — cheap Chinese capability increasingly comes with license terms that matter for enterprise deployment. - Agent infrastructure is a platform battleground. Google shipping an Apache 2.0 agent control plane signals the contested layer is moving from model to runtime, sandboxing and egress policy. - Policy is bifurcating. A U.S.–China incident notification channel and a UN loss-of-control brief landed in the same 24 hours as the largest chip vendor's CEO argued no new rules are needed.
