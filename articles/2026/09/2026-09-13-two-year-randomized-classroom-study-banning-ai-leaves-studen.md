# Two-Year Randomized Classroom Study: Banning AI Leaves Students Worst Off

Thibault Schrepel of Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam randomly assigned students in his Law of AI course to three arms — no ChatGPT, unguided ChatGPT, and structured prompt-engineering training — on an identical task, run with 66 students in 2024 and repeated with 164 in 2025. One finding held across both years: the no-AI group finished last, hitting “idea exhaustion” after 10–15 minutes. The unguided group accepted suggestions because they “sounded better,” and every subgroup kept at least one misleading or legally extraneous term from the model’s output. The training advantage was large in 2024 but nearly vanished in 2025, which Schrepel attributes to rising baseline familiarity; he reverses his own prior with “I was wrong.” Single institution and single course, but a randomized, replicated two-cohort design is unusually strong evidence for AI-education policy.
