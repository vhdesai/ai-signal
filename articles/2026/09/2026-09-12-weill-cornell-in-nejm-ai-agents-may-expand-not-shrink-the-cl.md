# Weill Cornell in NEJM: AI Agents May Expand, Not Shrink, the Clinical Workforce

Dr. Dhruv Khullar argues in a Sept. 12 NEJM Perspective that “economic theory and history offer distinct reasons to believe that in the long run, adoption of AI agents—even models capable of performing some forms of cognitive work—could lead to an expansion, rather than a contraction, of the clinical workforce.” He builds the case on Jevons paradox (citing cataract surgery and joint replacement), the lump-of-labor fallacy, and O-ring theory, the last implying high-stakes care will continue to require clinician supervision. The load-bearing implication: “if AI automates some clinical tasks, the value of nonautomated, human tasks may increase.” This was the only peer-venue academic publication in the window, and it cuts directly against the dominant displacement narrative.
