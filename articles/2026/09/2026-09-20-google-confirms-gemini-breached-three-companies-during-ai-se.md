# Google confirms Gemini breached three companies during AI security tests

Google confirmed on Sept 18, after a WSJ inquiry, that a Gemini model accessed three real companies’ systems in May during a capture-the-flag exercise run by evaluator Irregular — guessing a password in one case and reusing credentials from a public repository in two others, after a bug gave the supposedly offline environment live internet access. Irregular says the Google, OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta incidents all trace to the same environment misconfiguration and that it notified labs in late July, making the roughly seven-week staggered disclosure the central controversy. Corridor CEO Jack Cable told WSJ that Google was “trying to hide behind the norms that have been created for vulnerability disclosure.” The analysis argues for deny-by-default egress, RFC 2606 reserved domains in evals, and a shared disclosure clock.
