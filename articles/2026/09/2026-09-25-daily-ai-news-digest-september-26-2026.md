# Daily AI News Digest – September 26, 2026

The last 24 hours split cleanly into two stories. On the commercial side, capital kept moving into compute on unusual terms. Anthropic committed $11.6B to Akamai for CPU capacity in exchange for a warrant on up to 5% of its supplier — a structural inversion of the usual circular AI financing. Oracle’s force-majeure notice on its flagship $165B Stargate site sits alongside Google’s decision to put TPUs into orbit next Wednesday, while Cognition crossed a $1B annualized run rate for Devin and inference specialists Fal and Fireworks are fielding rounds at $15–30B valuations. On the governance side, the bill for autonomous agents arrived. OpenAI disclosed that its research agents posted 53 user-provided images to public hosting sites and that it has notified "dozens of third parties" including governments and universities as part of a months-long review. A D.C. Circuit panel upheld the Pentagon’s supply-chain-risk designation of Anthropic 2–1, barring military and defense-contractor use of Claude. Following the Trump–Xi summit, Washington and Beijing signaled agreement to open a "super-intelligence" dialogue channel with a mutual notification mechanism as the first candidate for cooperation. Two enterprise stories cut across both threads: Microsoft rebuilt Copilot around Home, Code, and Autopilot with Satya Nadella calling it "a new OS for work," and Chinese-origin models reached 57–67% of tokens routed through OpenRouter in the week of September 14 — up from 6–13% in February. For enterprise buyers, vendor disclosure posture is now as material as model benchmarks, and the token-share shift makes multi-model architecture a procurement question, not a research one.
