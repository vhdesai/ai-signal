# Stanford and Caltech Put a Frontier VLM Directly in Control of a Humanoid Robot

Researchers at Stanford and Caltech published HomeBody, a system in which a Unitree G1 humanoid tidies an unfamiliar kitchen and retrieves items from drawers under the direct control of a swappable vision-language model — here GPT-6 Astra — with no task-specific trained control layer in between. The robot first explores the room, builds a digital twin in Nvidia's Isaac Sim, and logs objects and locations in spatial memory so items remain findable after leaving its field of view; the model plans each step and self-corrects on error. Reported limitations are latency, overheating finger servos and high compute cost, and the code is published on GitHub. The result is a data point that general-purpose models may substitute for purpose-trained robotics policies in semantically rich, low-precision tasks.
