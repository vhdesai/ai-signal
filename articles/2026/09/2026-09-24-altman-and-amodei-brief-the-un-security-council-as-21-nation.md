# Altman and Amodei Brief the UN Security Council as 21 Nations Sign a Frontier-Model Control Appeal

Sam Altman (in person) and Dario Amodei (by video) addressed a 15-member UN Security Council session on artificial intelligence on September 23. Altman warned that global AI decision-making should be shaped by "democratic processes," not just "labs in San Francisco," and said OpenAI is not trying to "automate human judgment or human values." Amodei repeated his "pacing the frontier" recommendations, called for embedded external evaluators inside labs, and endorsed narrow international agreements such as banning AI use in biological-weapons development. Twenty-one countries including Australia, Canada, Germany, and Spain co-signed a "Call for Control of Frontier AI Models"; China’s UN ambassador Fu Cong called for stronger regulatory frameworks and cross-border emergency response, while US science adviser Michael Kratsios told the Council it should "focus on sharing best practices to build domestic capacity, not establishing a global regulatory scheme."
