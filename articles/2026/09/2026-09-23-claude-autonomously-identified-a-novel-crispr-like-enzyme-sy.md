# Claude autonomously identified a novel CRISPR-like enzyme system in bacteriophages

Anthropic introduced a life sciences research group and Bay Area wet lab, and disclosed that Claude agents identified a previously uncharacterized enzyme system it calls array-associated reverse transcriptases (ART), found in bacteriophages and combining a reverse transcriptase, a partner gene, and an evenly spaced DNA repeat array reminiscent of CRISPR. Roughly 950 agents ran about 21 hours on 210 million tokens, recovering more than 200,000 reverse transcriptases, scoring roughly 3,500 candidates and narrowing to 20 for lab work; human involvement was limited to the initial prompt and the bench experiments. MIT and Broad Institute CRISPR pioneer Feng Zhang, who reviewed the preprint, called it “genuinely intriguing” and worth further investigation, while outside microbiologists cautioned that a CRISPR-like array is not evidence of a rival gene-editing technology. The function of ART remains unknown and the work is not peer reviewed.
