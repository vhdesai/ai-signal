# The Information: Ginkgo’s "Mike vs. the Machines" Puts OpenAI Agents Against a Stanford Protein-Engineering Expert

Ginkgo Bioworks CEO Jason Kelly is producing a three-round contest at Ginkgo’s Boston autonomous lab in which Stanford’s Michael Jewett, an expert in cell-free protein synthesis, competes against OpenAI agents at protein design. Jewett may use any commercially available AI model; OpenAI’s team consists only of its most advanced, unreleased AI agents. Ginkgo executives judge; a documentary crew is filming. The framing is deliberate — Kasparov vs. Deep Blue, Lee Sedol vs. AlphaGo — and the underlying question is whether autonomous agents can now generate and validate novel biological designs at expert-scientist level. Coming the same week as Anthropic’s CRISPR-like ART pre-print, it is a concrete public benchmark for AI-in-the-wet-lab. Executive Takeaways - Vendor disclosure posture is now as material as model benchmarks. OpenAI’s 53-image research-agent leak, its notifications to "dozens of third parties," the D.C. Circuit’s upholding of the Pentagon’s Anthropic ban, and the Ginkgo-OpenAI biology contest sit in the same governance frame: labs are being asked to prove operational containment, not just capability. Bake incident-notification timelines, agent-egress controls, and independent-evaluator terms into every AI vendor contract signed this quarter. - Compute financing structures are inverting the flow of equity. Anthropic’s $11.6B Akamai commitment with a warrant on up to 5% of the supplier is the cleanest example of a customer being paid in equity to underwrite a supplier’s buildout. Combined with Fal at $15–20B and Fireworks at $30B, agentic orchestration is being financed as a distinct compute layer from GPU training — a new procurement category that CFOs and infrastructure architects should treat separately in 2027 budgeting. - Marquee AI-buildout cracks are visible and are power-driven. Oracle’s force-majeure on the $165B Stargate campus (project debt trading below 90 cents), the pending New Mexico air-quality decision on November 23, and Google’s decision to prototype orbital TPUs all point at the same binding constraint Goldman just called out. Treat contracted capacity and delivered capacity as separate diligence line items; watch credit spreads on hyperscaler bonds and neocloud project debt as early signals. - Chinese-origin models are winning on token share and voice pricing simultaneously. 57–67% of OpenRouter tokens and 55% of Vercel token share in a single reporting cycle, plus Qwen-Audio 3.1 with headline discounts up to 95% on ASR. Multi-model architecture is now a procurement-governance question — who approves which Chinese-origin models can be routed through developer tooling, and what data leaves your tenant — not a research-engineering question. - AI is compressing revenue models from the client side inward. DealBook’s law-firm story — clients using their own AI to line-item audit invoices and demand 20–30% markdowns, Norm AI marketing upfront pricing — is the leading indicator of what happens in every professional-services and SaaS relationship over the next 24 months. Microsoft’s Copilot restructure to usage-based pricing on Cowork/Code/Autopilot is the incumbent’s response. Assume your customers will do the same math on your line items; get ahead of it.
