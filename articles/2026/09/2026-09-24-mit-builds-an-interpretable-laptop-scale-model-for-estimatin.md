# MIT Builds an Interpretable, Laptop-Scale Model for Estimating Suicide Risk From Text

MIT researchers built a lexicon-based model covering roughly 60 words or phrases for each of 49 risk factors, then trained a lightweight classifier on about 16,000 de-identified Crisis Text Line conversations, reporting results in the Journal of Psychopathology and Clinical Science. The model predicted risk severity accurately on unseen conversations and found that mentions of lethal means and substance use were more likely among the highest-risk group than depressed mood or fatigue. It runs on a personal computer rather than GPU clusters and is explainable by construction. It is a deliberate counterpoint to LLM-based clinical risk scoring, with privacy and auditability as the design driver.
