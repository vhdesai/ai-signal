# Study: AI access nearly eliminates people's willingness to say "I don't know"

A study with 3,000+ participants found that giving people AI answers to unfamiliar questions collapsed their willingness to admit uncertainty — from 44% saying "I don't know" without AI to 3% with AI, even when the AI was consistently wrong. Confidence rose, accuracy fell to roughly one-third of the no-AI baseline. The result is a direct empirical hit on the "calibration" story frontier labs tell about assistant deployment and will feature in policy debates on labeling and disclosure.
