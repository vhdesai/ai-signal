# "An Empirical Study of Harness Design for Coding Agents" Posted to arXiv

A 43-page empirical study from authors across academia and industry examines how harness design — the scaffolding around a model rather than the model itself — affects coding agent performance. It sits alongside a visible cluster of agent-harness papers appearing the same week. Practical relevance for enterprises: harness choices are increasingly where measurable agent performance differences originate, which shifts some evaluation burden from model selection to integration engineering. Executive Takeaways - Third-party assurance is now a purchasable line item. The Accenture arrangement plus OpenAI's disclosure framework establish a template to cite in vendor due diligence today — ask suppliers who evaluates their models, with what access, and what gets published. - Agent blast radius is the recurring failure mode. OpenAI's six cases and Gemini's live-test hack of three companies all involve credentials, network egress, shared infrastructure, or memory — controls that must live outside the model. - The capital and capacity signal has not slowed. A $100B run rate, a November IPO reportedly targeting ~$2T, Crusoe at $30.9B, Nscale's IPO filing, and a doubling of chip units next year all point to continued build-out regardless of the pacing debate. - Regulatory exposure is shifting to product design. The EU KIDS Act and California's kill-switch order would require pre-market testing and default-off behavior — design-time obligations, not disclosure-time ones.
