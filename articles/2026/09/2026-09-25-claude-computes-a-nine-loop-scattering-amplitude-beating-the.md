# Claude computes a nine-loop scattering amplitude, beating the 2023 human record

Anthropic published a guest post by physicist Matt von Hippel reporting that Claude computed the six-particle scattering amplitude in planar N=4 super Yang-Mills at nine loops — one loop past the eight-loop record set by SLAC's Lance Dixon and Andy Liu in 2023, and a direct answer to a challenge von Hippel issued on August 7. The run used Fable 5.1 inside Claude Science, started from a one-sentence prompt, and proceeded largely unsupervised for days; the bootstrap step cost about $100 of compute (equivalent to 96 CPUs for a week), with either full route costing roughly $1,000–$2,000 end to end. Dixon independently verified the result via the related form factor. Von Hippel's own caveat is the important one for executives: Claude applied known methods with more compute and persistence, not new physics, and a human group in Beijing reached most of the same result in the same weeks.
