# UC Berkeley releases CUA-Lite, unifying sandboxes, data, evaluation and RL for computer-use agents

A Berkeley-led team open-sourced CUA-Lite, which unifies the four fragmented pieces of computer-use-agent work — agents, environments, traces and training/evaluation — behind a single action space and data schema across desktop, browser and mobile. Its headline contribution, Lite.OSWorld, reproduces the OSWorld benchmark VM-free inside a plain Docker container at 0.9 GB versus 4.1 GB, enabling roughly 4.6× more parallel desktops while matching OSWorld VM scores across 13 models. The release claims 30k+ verifiable tasks, 15+ integrated benchmarks, 10+ built-in agents and 20+ datasets on Hugging Face, with SFT and GRPO-based RL loops behind a single command. Coverage note: the named university blogs were checked and had no qualifying posts in the 24-hour window — MIT News AI last published Sept 2, Google Research Sept 3, Stanford HAI Aug 18, and the BAIR Blog Jul 29.
