# New Mexico Supreme Court fines attorney $5,000 over ChatGPT-fabricated witness testimony

The New Mexico Supreme Court fined Santa Fe attorney Stephen Aarons $5,000 and held him in contempt after an appellate brief in a murder case "contained false testimony from wholly fabricated witnesses," including invented police testimony and fictional details about the shooter's clothing. Aarons told the court he fed a computer-generated transcript into ChatGPT expecting "a bulletproof summary." The briefs were stricken, the appeal restarted, and the matter referred to the state disciplinary board. This escalates past the now-routine fake-citation cases: the fabrication went to the factual record, not the case law — a distinction any organization deploying AI in regulated workflows should note.
