# Johns Hopkins: LLMs Return Shorter, Weaker Writing for Woman-Coded Prompts — Adding a Male Name Doesn't Fix It

Johns Hopkins researchers — senior author Anjalie Field, lead Katherine Van Koevering — fed real workplace prompts (emails, job applications, resignation letters) into GPT-4, Llama, Gemma, and Mistral, adding linguistic features documented as woman-associated: hedging, collective phrasing, expressive adjectives. Every model returned shorter, less complex, lower-grade-level, and less formal correspondence for woman-coded prompts, and the gap persisted after controlling for tone mimicry. Adding a male name such as "John" had virtually no corrective effect. The paper — "It's How You Ask: Gender-Associated Linguistic Bias in LLMs" — will be presented at COLM in San Francisco, October 6–9, and is a direct fairness-assessment consideration for any enterprise deploying AI writing assistance.
