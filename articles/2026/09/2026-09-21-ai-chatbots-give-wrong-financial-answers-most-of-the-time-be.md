# AI chatbots give wrong financial answers most of the time, benchmark finds

The study tested 18 models across 121 money questions, each run five times — more than 10,000 scored responses — under a strict all-pass rubric. Average accuracy was 43%, falling to 12% on hard multi-part tax and figure scenarios; free models failed 63% of the time versus 49% for paid tiers, and the best overall result was Claude Opus 5 (reasoning) at 61%. In 6.1% of failures the model fabricated rules outright. The authors’ central point is that errors arrive in the same fluent, authoritative register as correct answers, and the weakest tier is the one used by people who cannot afford advisers.
