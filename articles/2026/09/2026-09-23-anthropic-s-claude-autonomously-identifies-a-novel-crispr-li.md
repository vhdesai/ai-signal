# Anthropic’s Claude Autonomously Identifies a Novel CRISPR-Like Enzyme System

Anthropic introduced a life-sciences research group and a Bay Area BSL-1/BSL-2 wet lab, and disclosed that Claude agents autonomously identified a previously uncharacterized bacteriophage enzyme system it calls array-associated reverse transcriptases (ART) — a reverse transcriptase paired with an accessory protein and an evenly spaced non-coding DNA repeat array structurally reminiscent of CRISPR. Roughly 950 agents ran 21 hours on 210 million tokens, collecting more than 200,000 reverse transcriptases, narrowing to ~3,500 candidate systems, and writing up 20 for human review; humans supplied only the initial prompt and performed all lab work. MIT/Broad’s Feng Zhang called the finding "genuinely intriguing." ART’s function remains unknown and the work is a pre-print — the signal is throughput on hypothesis generation, not a validated tool. CEO Dario Amodei framed the result as evidence that "AI performance in new intellectual domains goes from weak to superhuman in a matter of a few years." Academic Research ACADEMIC RESEARCH
