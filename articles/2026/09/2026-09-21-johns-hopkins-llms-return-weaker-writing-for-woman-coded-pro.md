# Johns Hopkins: LLMs Return Weaker Writing for Woman-Coded Prompts

Johns Hopkins researchers — senior author Anjalie Field, lead author Katherine Van Koevering — fed real workplace prompts including emails, job applications and resignation letters into GPT-4, Llama, Gemma and Mistral, adding linguistic features documented as woman-associated such as hedging, collective phrasing and expressive adjectives. Every model returned shorter, less complex, lower-grade-level and less formal correspondence for woman-coded prompts, and the gap persisted after controlling for tone mimicry. Adding a male name such as "John" had virtually no corrective effect. The paper, "It's How You Ask: Gender-Associated Linguistic Bias in LLMs" (arXiv, DOI 10.48550/arxiv.2608.13328), will be presented at COLM in San Francisco, October 6–9 — a direct fairness-assessment consideration for any enterprise deploying AI writing assistance.
