# $500K in grants funds non-transformer architecture research at Stanford, MIT, Berkeley, UT Austin and Yale

Five $100,000 awards, selected from nearly 100 submissions, went to faculty pursuing deliberate departures from the transformer-and-scaling recipe: retention-aware AI processors treating memory lifetime as an architected resource (Thierry Tambe, Stanford); testing whether reasoning-model slowdowns are transient chaos producing fractal traps in latent space (William Gilpin, UT Austin); physical and analog neural scaling laws (Logan Wright, Yale); pushing looped transformers past the tens of loops where gains saturate into the hundreds and thousands (Yoon Kim, MIT); and brain-inspired recurrent architectures for efficient state tracking (UC Berkeley). The memory-wall and state-tracking projects target exactly the cost structure — HBM capacity and quadratic context cost — that dominates inference bills today, though on a three-to-five-year horizon.
