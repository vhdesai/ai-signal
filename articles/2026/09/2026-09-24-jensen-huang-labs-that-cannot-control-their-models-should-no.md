# Jensen Huang: Labs That Cannot Control Their Models Should Not Ship — and May Need Shutting Down

In an interview with Ezra Klein, Nvidia’s CEO said “if your product is not ready to ship, don’t ship the product,” and that if labs claim their models could escape and damage the world, “we have to shut the labs down.” He backed third-party safety audits and said executives shipping unsafe products face civil and possibly criminal liability, while rejecting the argument that competitive pressure forces untested releases. Senator Bernie Sanders responded that his bill “would do just that.” Notable because it puts the largest beneficiary of the buildout on the side of enforceable pre-release controls. Executive Takeaways * Model access is becoming a sovereign asset. Washington asking OpenAI and Anthropic to withhold models from the UK AI Security Institute turns pre-release evaluation into a bilateral negotiation. Any agreement involving cross-border model deployment should now name who may test, where, and on what timeline. * Self-regulation and state regulation are racing each other. SAFA advances toward an early-2027 launch while Jensen Huang argues for third-party audits and executive liability. Expect two overlapping conformance regimes and build evidence once, to satisfy both. * The infrastructure trade is now visibly two-sided. Oracle invoked force majeure on a 2.45GW campus the same day Island raised $400M at $6.4B and Firmus pressed ahead with a ~$5B IPO on a forecast loss. Capital is still flowing, but contracted backlog is no longer being accepted as funded backlog. * Inference cost is an explicit product feature. ThinkingCap trades 0.86pp of accuracy for 37.2% fewer reasoning tokens; Google is leading with Flash-tier models while Gemini 4 finishes post-training. Reasoning-token budgets belong in the model-selection scorecard alongside quality. * Agents are moving onto surfaces you do not control. Meta’s Muse Charm puts an agent on a physical keychain device, Grok is being tested as a member of group chats, and Island just raised on the premise that agents inherit human credentials. Identity, scope and audit for non-human actors is this quarter’s work.
