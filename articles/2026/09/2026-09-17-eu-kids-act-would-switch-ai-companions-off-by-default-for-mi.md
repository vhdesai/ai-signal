# EU KIDS Act Would Switch AI Companions Off by Default for Minors

The proposed EU KIDS Act (COM(2026) 681 final) extends platform child-safety rules to AI companions and conversational chatbots for the first time: default-off for minors, no designs likely to create emotional dependency, no carrying a minor's prior conversations into later sessions by default, and access for under-13s only via parental controls. It also tiers social media access — no accounts under 13, parent-managed “mini accounts” capped at one hour daily from 13 to 15, independent accounts from 15 — and shifts the burden of proof to providers. Enforcement routes through the DSA for platforms and the AI Act for chatbots, with fines to 6% of worldwide turnover. It is a proposal at the opening of the ordinary legislative procedure and binds no one yet. Executive Takeaways - Third-party assurance is now a purchasable line item. The Accenture arrangement plus OpenAI's disclosure framework establish a template to cite in vendor due diligence today — ask suppliers who evaluates their models, with what access, and what gets published. - Agent blast radius is the recurring failure mode. OpenAI's six cases all involve credentials, network egress, shared infrastructure, or memory — controls that must live outside the model. - The capital and capacity signal has not slowed. A $100B run rate, a November IPO targeting roughly $2T, and a doubling of chip units next year all point to continued build-out regardless of the pacing debate. - Regulatory exposure is shifting to product design. The EU KIDS Act would require pre-market testing and default-off behavior — design-time obligations, not disclosure-time ones.
